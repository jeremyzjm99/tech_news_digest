The agent security gap: 54% of enterprises have already had an AI agent incident, and most still let agents share credentials

A June 2026 survey of 107 enterprises found that over half have already experienced an AI agent security incident or near-miss, yet most organizations lack basic controls: only one-third assign agents their own scoped credentials, just 30% isolate high-risk agents in sandboxes, and security tooling remains dominated by provider-native guardrails from OpenAI, Google, and Microsoft rather than purpose-built solutions. Despite high satisfaction scores with current tooling, budgets remain thin and only a third of enterprises believe their defenses are outpacing AI-enabled attackers. A clear majority plan to replace or augment their security stack within a year, though the identity and isolation controls most needed are still largely absent from purchase plans.

The AI compute gap: Enterprises are buying infrastructure faster than they can measure what it costs

A Q2 2026 survey of 107 enterprises found that AI infrastructure spending is accelerating well ahead of organizations' ability to measure or manage its costs — a dynamic researchers call the 'compute gap.' Most enterprises run AI on hyperscalers and model APIs today, yet 64% plan to switch or add providers within a year, with AI-specialized clouds topping evaluation lists despite near-zero current adoption. Meanwhile, 83% report GPU utilization at 50% or below, and fewer than half rigorously track what their compute actually costs, meaning organizations are buying more infrastructure while remaining largely blind to the economics of what they already own.

The AI context gap: Enterprise AI organizations have a trust problem, not a retrieval problem — and most are still building the fix

A Q2 2026 survey of 101 enterprises found that 57% have experienced AI agents producing confident but incorrect answers due to missing or inconsistent business context, with RAG being the primary context source for most organizations. Provider-native retrieval tools from OpenAI and Google already outpace dedicated vector databases in production usage, yet a plurality of enterprises say they prefer to maintain best-of-breed standalone tools. The emerging fix—a governed semantic layer with hybrid retrieval—is under active development at most organizations but remains largely pre-production, leaving a significant 'context gap' between how authoritatively agents respond and how reliable their underlying data foundations actually are.

The agent evaluation gap: Enterprise AI organizations have a reality-alignment problem, not a coverage problem — and most are shipping to production anyway

A June 2026 survey of 157 enterprises found that half have shipped an AI agent that passed internal evaluations but then failed in production, yet two-thirds are moving toward fully automated, no-human-in-the-loop deployments anyway. Trust in automated evaluation is extremely low—only 5% fully trust it—with the top complaint being that evals don't reflect real-world outcomes, and most production monitoring tracks system uptime rather than output correctness. The evaluation tooling market remains fragmented and immature, with provider-native tools and no dedicated tooling tied as the most common approaches, even as 64% of organizations plan to switch or adopt new platforms within a year.
